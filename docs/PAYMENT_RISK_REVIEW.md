# Payment risk review

October 27, 2026 · Asia/Kuala_Lumpur · localhost only

The existing PaymentEvidence/PaymentReview workflow is extended; there is no new payment engine. Receipts are privately stored unchanged. Submission fingerprints original bytes with SHA-256, stores an optional integer-sen claimed amount and recipient name, and stores only a digest of a normalized optional payment reference. Filenames are never a duplicate key. Reference normalization uses NFKC, uppercase and whitespace removal; the reference itself is not displayed/exported or logged. This deliberately favors a private review signal rather than uniqueness enforcement.

Signals: identical receipt, reused supplied reference, amount mismatch, recipient mismatch, multiple rejected submissions, repeated corrections, evidence outside the original reservation window, original destination no longer eligible, inactive booking and historical evidence not yet fingerprinted. Already-inactive/expired unheld bookings still reject uploads under existing rules. On-time evidence held for review can accept a later correction, flagged against the original window. Disabling a destination never rewrites the original recipient snapshot or invents a refund. A superseded but still valid approved historical version is not itself proof of wrongdoing.

Warnings say POSSIBLE DUPLICATE or NEEDS REVIEW. A legitimate group/shared payment may trigger them. Matching or clean evidence cannot confirm money, reject a customer, grant registration approval or issue a ticket. Finance-authorized reviewers must independently attest incoming funds, enter the exact amount and review notes; registration/BYOB checks stay separate. Existing atomic/idempotent financial and ticket gates remain authoritative.

Organizers/finance staff analyze only their organizer scope. Administrators can run platform-wide analysis. Responses contain only the selected booking's evidence/claims and review history, generic indicator labels and scope; no matching customer identity, booking ID, receipt URL or digest. Cross-organizer matches cannot grant receipt access. Scanner-only staff and customers are denied financial risk endpoints. Private receipt authorization is unchanged.

Submission records risk types through existing audit rows. Same-organizer duplicate evidence also receives a stored marker, making earlier submissions findable in the recorded-risk filter. At most 1,000 prior matches are touched in a submission transaction; later repeated duplicates already acquire their own marker. Dynamic detail review recomputes current signals, including destination/lifecycle changes. The recorded-risk reconciliation filter is not a claim of a real-time global fraud registry. Review decisions retain reviewer, time, notes, amount and independent-funds flag; a risk-review audit records observed codes and decision. Raw credentials/banking passwords/ticket secrets are never stored in these audit fields.

The local-only guarded backfill reads 10 legacy evidence files and 200 booking names per page in the calling Phase9 workspace. It fingerprints old bytes and indexes the previously authorized holder name without modifying historical financial/recipient/answer fields. Never run it against production; a later production upgrade needs its own reviewed operational plan. Historical unavailable bytes require manual verification. Exact-byte SHA-256 does not detect edited/re-encoded/cropped receipts or verify bank settlement.

Evidence: tests/phase9.test.ts, phase9/browser-supplement.json, provider TAP logs, retained baseline financial/concurrency tests. All identities, payments and approvals are fictional.
